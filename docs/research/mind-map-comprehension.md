# Mind-map properties that aid comprehension

Research for [#21](https://github.com/theRealNG/learn_with_mindmaps/issues/21) (child of map [#20](https://github.com/theRealNG/learn_with_mindmaps/issues/20)).
Glossary terms (**Map**, **Node**, **Link**, **Slop**, **Source**, **Document**) as in `CONTEXT.md` and the map issue.

**Question.** What makes a mind map good to write and easy to read? What does the evidence say about structure, Node labels, ordering, cross-branch Links, visual encoding, Map vs. outline/prose, and failure modes? The answer ends in a ranked list of candidate Map-quality rules.

## How this was researched (and its limits)

- Sources, roughly in order of weight: meta-analyses and controlled experiments in educational and cognitive psychology; controlled experiments in information visualisation and HCI; first-party method documents (Novak & Cañas for concept maps); practitioner guidance (Buzan, Nielsen Norman Group), which is labelled as such wherever it is used.
- **Limitation:** the sandbox's egress proxy blocked full-text fetches from publisher and repository hosts (sfu.ca, ihmc.us, eric.ed.gov, PMC). Every claim below was checked against the source's abstract or an indexed summary of it through web search, and each one links to the primary record. Effect sizes not in the abstract are marked *(verify in full text)*.
- **Evidence gap that matters for us:** almost all of this research measures **learning** (recall, transfer) by students. None of it measures **triage** by knowledge workers ("what's in this Document, and does it matter to me?"). For triage, the closest evidence is HCI work on scanning and navigation (information scent, menu breadth/depth). The fixture-set eval and the user studies listed under "Not yet specified" in #20 are the only way to close this gap.

## 1. Map vs. outline vs. prose: does a Map help, and for whom?

| Finding | Source | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Studying or building node-link maps improves retention compared with reading text passages, lectures and discussion. The benefit holds across 55 studies (5,818 participants, Grade 4 to postsecondary). Effects run from small to large depending on how the map is used and what it is compared with. | [Nesbit & Adesope 2006, *Rev. Educ. Res.* 76(3)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/00346543076003413) ([PDF](http://www.sfu.ca/~jcnesbit/articles/NesbitAdesope2006.pdf)) | Strong (meta-analysis) |
| The update: 142 effect sizes, n = 11,814. Overall g = 0.58. Building maps g = 0.72, **studying a supplied map g = 0.43**. Works in both STEM and non-STEM domains. | [Schroeder, Nesbit, Anguiano & Adesope 2018, *Educ. Psychol. Rev.* 30](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-017-9403-9) | Strong (meta-analysis) |
| Maps beat outlines and passages, but the edge over *other summarising strategies* (writing lists or outlines) is small, around g ≈ 0.19 *(verify in full text)*. | [Nesbit & Adesope 2006](http://www.sfu.ca/~jcnesbit/articles/NesbitAdesope2006.pdf) | Strong / moderate |
| **Individual differences:** learners with low verbal ability gain more from maps than from text. Learners with high verbal ability showed no benefit, or did better with text (reported d ≈ +0.44 vs. −0.33) *(verify in full text)*. | [Nesbit & Adesope 2006](http://www.sfu.ca/~jcnesbit/articles/NesbitAdesope2006.pdf); review in [O'Donnell, Dansereau & Hall 2002, *Educ. Psychol. Rev.* 14](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1013132527007) | Moderate |
| Given enough time, graphic organisers beat outlines and text for learning **hierarchical *and* coordinate (cross-topic) relations**, and for writing integrated essays. The benefit comes from relations that span branches, which an outline hides. | [Robinson & Kiewra 1995, *J. Educ. Psychol.* 87(3)](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ517168) | Moderate (2 experiments, n = 153) |
| Buzan-style *mind maps* (radial, keyword, unlabelled associations): one RCT found better long-term factual recall (n = 50) but **lower motivation**. Another found no difference from standard note-taking in recall or critical thinking (n = 131). | [Farrand, Hussain & Hennessy 2002, *Med. Educ.* 36](https://asmepublications.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1046/j.1365-2923.2002.01205.x); [D'Antoni et al. 2010, *BMC Med. Educ.* 10:61](https://bmcmededuc.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/1472-6920-10-61) | Weak / mixed |

**Takeaways for us.**
- The robust evidence is for **concept maps / knowledge maps**, which are node-link diagrams with **labelled** relations. Buzan-style mind maps have only weak evidence. This supports #20's decision that Maps carry labelled **Links**: they are the part with evidence behind it.
- Reading a supplied Map works, but less well than building one (0.43 vs. 0.72). Our readers *receive* Maps, so expect moderate gains, not large ones.
- The benefit concentrates in readers with lower verbal ability or less prior knowledge, and in **relations across topics**. Strong verbal readers may do as well with text. This supports keeping the **outline view** (already decided) as a first-class alternative.

## 2. Structure: hierarchy, depth, breadth, size

- **Working-memory limits.** Miller's "7 ± 2" ([Miller 1956, *Psychol. Rev.*](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/10255186_The_magical_number_seven_plus_or_minus_two_some_limits_on_our_capacity_for_processing_information)) has been revised to about **3–5 chunks** when rehearsal and chunking are blocked ([Cowan 2001, *Behav. Brain Sci.* 24(1), doi:10.1017/S0140525X01003922](https://philpapers.org/rec/COWTMN)). Cognitive load theory builds instructional design on this limited working memory: reduce *extraneous* load and help schema building ([Sweller 1988, *Cognitive Science* 12(2)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1207/s15516709cog1202_4); [Sweller, van Merriënboer & Paas 1998, *Educ. Psychol. Rev.* 10](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1022193728205)). Implication: the set of siblings a reader must *compare and hold* at once (especially the Root Nodes) should be about 3–5, and 7 at most. *Strong for the limit itself; the jump to "siblings per Node" is an inference.*
- **Breadth vs. depth when the items are visible.** When items stay on screen (navigation, not recall), shallow-and-broad beats deep. With 512 leaves, a 2-level 16×32 hierarchy was fastest and a 3-level 8×8×8 slowest. **Distinct category names at the top levels** mattered most ([Larson & Czerwinski 1998, CHI](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/221518519_Web_Page_Design_Implications_of_Memory_Structure_and_Scent_for_Information_Retrieval)). Implication: avoid needless depth. Depth costs more than breadth *when siblings can be scanned*, but top-level categories must be clearly distinct. *Moderate (navigation task, not comprehension).*
- **Total size per view.** Novak & Cañas say 15–25 concepts usually suffice for a concept map built around one focus question ([Novak & Cañas, IHMC Tech. Rep. 2006-01 rev. 2008](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~paritosh/papers/sketch-to-models/Novak-Canas-TheoryUnderlyingConceptMapsHQ.pdf)). Node-link diagrams lose readability against matrices once graphs pass about **20 vertices** on most tasks; only path-finding stays in favour of node-link ([Ghoniem, Fekete & Castagliola 2004, InfoVis](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/A-Comparison-of-the-Readability-of-Graphs-Using-and-Ghoniem-Fekete/4effb869de6edd5e6e7e1ec476ab402fa9d28c55)). Large static maps cause **"map shock"**: readers feel overwhelmed and lose motivation ([Blankenship & Dansereau 2000, *J. Exp. Educ.* 68(4)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/249038782_The_Effect_of_Animated_Node-Link_Displays_on_Information_Recall)). Implication: a whole-Document Map can be large (coverage is the promise), but **any single view** should show about 15–25 Nodes. *Moderate.*
- **Hierarchical beats network for novices.** Learners with low prior knowledge who studied a hierarchical concept map gained more conceptual knowledge, with less mental effort, than those given a network-structured map ([Amadieu, van Gog, Paas, Tricot & Mariné 2009, *Learn. Instr.* 19](https://research.ou.nl/ws/files/1022307/Amadieu-etal_LI_2009.pdf)). Novak & Cañas also prescribe most-general-at-top hierarchy ([IHMC report](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~paritosh/papers/sketch-to-models/Novak-Canas-TheoryUnderlyingConceptMapsHQ.pdf)). Implication: the **tree is the backbone and Links sit on top of it**, which is our model already. *Moderate–strong.*
- **Balanced vs. lopsided trees.** No direct evidence was found. Indirectly, salient spatial grouping of related concepts reduces disorientation ([Krieglstein et al. 2022, *ETR&D* 70](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11423-022-10083-2)), and gestalt-symmetric layouts help ([Wiegmann et al. 1992, *Contemp. Educ. Psychol.* 17, as reviewed in O'Donnell et al. 2002](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1013132527007)). A lopsided tree is fine if it mirrors the Source's real emphasis. Faithfulness beats symmetry. *Practitioner / inference.*

## 3. Node labels

- **Minimum words; a noun phrase for the concept, a verb for the relation.** "Each concept consists of the minimum number of words needed to express the object or event, and linking words are also as concise as possible and usually include a verb." Concept + linking phrase + concept forms a **proposition**, the unit of meaning ([Novak & Cañas](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~paritosh/papers/sketch-to-models/Novak-Canas-TheoryUnderlyingConceptMapsHQ.pdf); [IHMC "What is a concept map"](https://cmap.ihmc.us/docs/conceptmap.php)). *First-party method source; it shaped the studies in §1.*
- **One keyword per branch** (Buzan) is the more extreme version. Its rationale ("a word triggers associations, a sentence closes them") is aimed at *the author's* brainstorming, not at a reader decoding someone else's map ([summary of Buzan's rules](https://www.mind-mapping.org/index.php?title=Tony_Buzan_has_defined_these_%22rules%22_for_Mind_Mapping)). For a reader who never saw the Source, single keywords are ambiguous. *Practitioner-only; we should not adopt it literally.* Our Node has a **label plus a summary**, so the label can stay short (about 1–5 words) and the summary can carry the full proposition.
- **Front-load the information-bearing words.** Readers scanning lists fixate on the first words. A label should still give the gist if only its first two words are read ([NN/g, F-shaped reading](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content/); [NN/g, headings](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/headings-pickup-lines/)). Labels act as **information scent**: readers choose where to spend attention based on how well cues predict the content behind them ([Pirolli & Card 1999, *Psychol. Rev.* 106(4)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/information-foraging/)). Generic labels ("Overview", "Key considerations", "Other") give no scent. They are Slop in label form. *Moderate (HCI theory and eye-tracking; practitioner rules).*
- **Distinct sibling labels.** Distinct category names at top levels drove navigation success ([Larson & Czerwinski 1998](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/221518519_Web_Page_Design_Implications_of_Memory_Structure_and_Scent_for_Information_Retrieval)). *Moderate.*
- **Parallel phrasing** (siblings with the same grammatical form): no primary experimental evidence was found. It is standard style guidance and plausibly lowers parsing cost. *Practitioner-only.*

## 4. Ordering

- **Most general and most important first.** Concept maps put the most inclusive concepts at the top and the specific ones below ([Novak & Cañas](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~paritosh/papers/sketch-to-models/Novak-Canas-TheoryUnderlyingConceptMapsHQ.pdf)). Under progressive disclosure, what appears first signals importance ([NN/g, progressive disclosure](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/)). *Moderate / practitioner.*
- **Radial reading order is ambiguous.** In an eye-tracking study, people found the least common ancestor much faster in traditional and orthogonal tree layouts than in radial ones. Radial layouts caused cross-checking behaviour. Layouts **with the root on top performed best and were liked most** ([Burch et al. 2011, *IEEE TVCG* 17(12)](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/6065011/)). Radial maps have no natural start point or reading direction. Linear trees (top-down, or left-to-right for wide labels) do: siblings read top-to-bottom, matching outline order. *Moderate (one well-run controlled study, structural task).*

## 5. Cross-links (our **Link**)

- **Why they matter.** Cross-links "help to illustrate how these domains are related to one another" and are the key evidence that the relationships between sub-domains are understood ([Novak & Cañas](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~paritosh/papers/sketch-to-models/Novak-Canas-TheoryUnderlyingConceptMapsHQ.pdf)). The advantage of graphic organisers over outlines lies in **coordinate / cross-topic relations** ([Robinson & Kiewra 1995](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ517168)).
- **Labelled links are what the evidence covers.** The node-link maps in the meta-analyses are concept or knowledge maps with labelled links ([O'Donnell et al. 2002](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1013132527007)). Link labels should be short verb phrases that make a readable proposition ("caching *reduces* latency") ([Novak & Cañas](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~paritosh/papers/sketch-to-models/Novak-Canas-TheoryUnderlyingConceptMapsHQ.pdf)). Knowledge-map research uses a **small fixed vocabulary of relation types** (e.g. *part of, type of, leads to, influences, characteristic, example*), which readers learn to recognise ([O'Donnell et al. 2002](https://scholarsmine.mst.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1470&context=bio_inftec_facwork)). *Moderate.*
- **How many is too many? The evidence is in tension, which suggests a split.**
  - *Content side:* more interconnections in a concept map **reduced** extraneous cognitive load, with no change in intrinsic or germane load (n = 81). Links act as structure, not clutter ([Krieglstein et al. 2025, *Instr. Sci.*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11251-025-09736-5)). *Moderate (single study).*
  - *Rendering side:* **edge crossings are by far the most damaging layout aesthetic** for graph comprehension ([Purchase 1997, Graph Drawing](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/3-540-63938-1_67)). Large node-link displays cause map shock ([Blankenship & Dansereau 2000](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/249038782_The_Effect_of_Animated_Node-Link_Displays_on_Information_Recall)). Network (non-hierarchical) structure costs novices more effort ([Amadieu et al. 2009](https://research.ou.nl/ws/files/1022307/Amadieu-etal_LI_2009.pdf)). *Strong for crossings; moderate otherwise.*
  - **Resolution:** the generator should emit **every Link that states a real, non-obvious relation in the Source**, and none that only restate the tree. The **renderer** controls clutter: bundle Links along the hierarchy ([Holten 2006, *IEEE TVCG* 12(5)](https://www.cs.jhu.edu/~misha/ReadingSeminar/Papers/Holten06.pdf)), show them on focus or hover, and draw them only between visible Nodes. No study gives a numeric Link budget.

## 6. Visual encoding and disclosure

- **Layout.** Use a traditional or orthogonal tree with the root on top or left, not radial ([Burch et al. 2011](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/6065011/)). Make related concepts spatially salient as groups: low structural salience increased disorientation ([Krieglstein et al. 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11423-022-10083-2)). Gestalt-designed maps (symmetry, proximity, good continuation) had a strong advantage ([Wiegmann et al. 1992, via O'Donnell et al. 2002](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1013132527007)). Minimise crossings ([Purchase 1997](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/3-540-63938-1_67)). *Moderate–strong.*
- **Signalling.** Cues that highlight how the material is organised improve retention (g = 0.53) and transfer (g = 0.33) and lower cognitive load across 103 studies. **Prior knowledge did not moderate the effect**, so it helps experts too ([Schneider, Beege, Nebel & Rey 2018, *Educ. Res. Rev.* 23](https://www.learntechlib.org/p/204443/)). Emphasis (weight, size, colour) on Root Nodes and branch identity is a signal. *Strong.*
- **Colour.** Categorical (nominal) colour coding is effective for separating groups ([Ware, *Information Visualization: Perception for Design*](https://scholars.unh.edu/ccom/143/)). Only about **7 colours** stay rapidly and reliably distinguishable from each other ([Healey 1996](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/2639665_Choosing_Effective_Colours_for_Data_Visualization)). Buzan's "one colour per main branch" fits this ([Buzan rules](https://www.mind-mapping.org/index.php?title=Tony_Buzan_has_defined_these_%22rules%22_for_Mind_Mapping)), but his neuro-hemispheric rationale is not evidence. Implication: one hue per Root branch, which also caps colour-coded roots at about 7. *Moderate.*
- **Progressive disclosure.** "Overview first, zoom and filter, then details-on-demand" ([Shneiderman 1996](https://hci.stanford.edu/courses/cs448b/papers/shneiderman96eyes.pdf)). Defer secondary detail so the first view shows what matters ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/)). Animated, stepwise reveal of node-link maps was proposed to counter map shock, but it fails when the reader cannot control the pace ([Blankenship & Dansereau 2000](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/249038782_The_Effect_of_Animated_Node-Link_Displays_on_Information_Recall); discussion in [SFU thesis on dynamic concept maps](https://summit.sfu.ca/_flysystem/fedora/sfu_migrate/9884/etd1833.pdf)). Implication: reveal is **reader-driven** (expand/collapse), never auto-played. *Moderate.*
- **Decoration.** Removing extraneous material improves learning: Mayer's coherence principle held in 23 of 23 tests, median d = 0.86 ([Mayer, *Cambridge Handbook of Multimedia Learning*, ch. on extraneous processing](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-handbook-of-multimedia-learning/principles-for-reducing-extraneous-processing-in-multimedia-learning/F29A19FCD34C542806F736E0661C05F5)). The seductive-details meta-analysis agrees ([Sundararajan & Adesope 2020, cited in Noetel et al. 2022](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.3102/00346543211052329)). This argues against Buzan's images and curved decorative branches as default renderer features. It is also the strongest evidence-based argument for **clearing Slop** in the first place. *Strong.*
- **Expertise reversal.** Scaffolds that help novices can hurt experts ([Kalyuga, Ayres, Chandler & Sweller 2003, *Educ. Psychol.* 38](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-007-9054-3)). Together with the verbal-ability interaction in §1, this argues for letting expert triagers collapse to a terse view or switch to the outline. *Strong (effect); moderate (application to Maps).*

## 7. Failure modes (what makes Maps hard to read)

1. **Too big in one view.** Map shock and node-link breakdown past about 20 Nodes ([Blankenship & Dansereau 2000](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/249038782_The_Effect_of_Animated_Node-Link_Displays_on_Information_Recall); [Ghoniem et al. 2004](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/A-Comparison-of-the-Readability-of-Graphs-Using-and-Ghoniem-Fekete/4effb869de6edd5e6e7e1ec476ab402fa9d28c55)).
2. **Crossing Links / spaghetti** ([Purchase 1997](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/3-540-63938-1_67)).
3. **Weak spatial grouping**, which disorients readers ([Krieglstein et al. 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11423-022-10083-2)).
4. **Network-first structure for novices** ([Amadieu et al. 2009](https://research.ou.nl/ws/files/1022307/Amadieu-etal_LI_2009.pdf)).
5. **Vague or overlapping labels** with no information scent ([Larson & Czerwinski 1998](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/221518519_Web_Page_Design_Implications_of_Memory_Structure_and_Scent_for_Information_Retrieval); [Pirolli & Card 1999](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/information-foraging/)).
6. **Unlabelled or vague Links** ("related to"), which don't form a proposition ([Novak & Cañas](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~paritosh/papers/sketch-to-models/Novak-Canas-TheoryUnderlyingConceptMapsHQ.pdf)).
7. **Needless depth**, where every extra level costs a navigation step ([Larson & Czerwinski 1998](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/221518519_Web_Page_Design_Implications_of_Memory_Structure_and_Scent_for_Information_Retrieval)).
8. **Decoration and seductive detail** ([Mayer coherence principle](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-handbook-of-multimedia-learning/principles-for-reducing-extraneous-processing-in-multimedia-learning/F29A19FCD34C542806F736E0661C05F5)).
9. **Radial layout with no reading direction** ([Burch et al. 2011](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/6065011/)).
10. **Forcing the Map on strong verbal readers** with no text alternative ([Nesbit & Adesope 2006](http://www.sfu.ca/~jcnesbit/articles/NesbitAdesope2006.pdf)).

## Candidate Map-quality rules (ranked)

Evidence tags: **strong** = meta-analysis or a replicated, well-established effect; **moderate** = one or a few controlled studies, or a strong effect applied by inference; **practitioner-only** = method guides or UX practice with no direct experiment found. Rank combines evidence strength with expected impact on our primary reader, the knowledge worker triaging a Document.

### Generator rules (content, structure, labels, Links)

| # | Rule | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| G1 | **No Slop in Nodes.** Every Node states content from the Source. Drop restatement, framing and filler; coherence beats completeness-by-padding. | Strong ([Mayer coherence](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-handbook-of-multimedia-learning/principles-for-reducing-extraneous-processing-in-multimedia-learning/F29A19FCD34C542806F736E0661C05F5)) |
| G2 | **Hierarchy first.** Build a most-general to most-specific tree as the backbone, then add Links on top. Never a flat network. | Moderate–strong ([Amadieu 2009](https://research.ou.nl/ws/files/1022307/Amadieu-etal_LI_2009.pdf); [Novak & Cañas](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~paritosh/papers/sketch-to-models/Novak-Canas-TheoryUnderlyingConceptMapsHQ.pdf)) |
| G3 | **3–5 Root Nodes (hard max 7).** Siblings that must be compared fit in working memory. Split or group if there are more. | Strong for the limit, moderate as applied ([Cowan 2001](https://philpapers.org/rec/COWTMN); [Miller 1956](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/10255186_The_magical_number_seven_plus_or_minus_two_some_limits_on_our_capacity_for_processing_information)) |
| G4 | **Emit labelled cross-branch Links for every real relation the Source states that the tree does not show.** Each Link's label is a short verb phrase that makes a proposition when read *source → label → target*. | Moderate ([Robinson & Kiewra 1995](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ517168); [Novak & Cañas](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~paritosh/papers/sketch-to-models/Novak-Canas-TheoryUnderlyingConceptMapsHQ.pdf); [Krieglstein 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11251-025-09736-5)) |
| G5 | **No redundant or vague Links.** No Link that duplicates a parent–child edge, joins two siblings for the obvious reason, or is labelled "related to" / "see also". | Moderate (inference from G4 plus [Purchase 1997](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/3-540-63938-1_67) clutter cost) |
| G6 | **Short, front-loaded Node labels.** About 1–5 words, a noun phrase naming the concept, with the information-bearing word first. The **summary** carries the full statement, so labels never need to be sentences. | Moderate ([Novak & Cañas](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~paritosh/papers/sketch-to-models/Novak-Canas-TheoryUnderlyingConceptMapsHQ.pdf); [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content/); [Pirolli & Card](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/information-foraging/)) |
| G7 | **Specific, mutually distinct sibling labels.** No generic buckets ("Overview", "Other", "Key considerations"). Siblings must not overlap in meaning. This matters most at the top level. | Moderate ([Larson & Czerwinski 1998](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/221518519_Web_Page_Design_Implications_of_Memory_Structure_and_Scent_for_Information_Retrieval)) |
| G8 | **Order siblings by importance to the Document's main point**, most important first. Use the Source's own order only when it is a sequence (steps, timeline). | Practitioner-only ([Novak & Cañas](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~paritosh/papers/sketch-to-models/Novak-Canas-TheoryUnderlyingConceptMapsHQ.pdf); [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/)) |
| G9 | **Prefer shallow over deep.** Add a level only when it groups siblings into distinct categories; never wrap a single child. | Moderate ([Larson & Czerwinski 1998](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/221518519_Web_Page_Design_Implications_of_Memory_Structure_and_Scent_for_Information_Retrieval)) |
| G10 | **Small, fixed vocabulary of Link relation types** (e.g. *causes, enables, contradicts, is example of, depends on, part of*) with free-text verb labels inside each type. | Moderate–practitioner ([O'Donnell et al. 2002](https://scholarsmine.mst.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1470&context=bio_inftec_facwork)) |
| G11 | **Answer the Document's focus question.** The Root level answers "what is this Document claiming / for?" | Practitioner-only ([Novak & Cañas focus question](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~paritosh/papers/sketch-to-models/Novak-Canas-TheoryUnderlyingConceptMapsHQ.pdf)) |
| G12 | **Parallel phrasing among siblings** (same grammatical form). | Practitioner-only |
| G13 | **Let tree shape follow the Source's emphasis.** Don't pad thin branches or trim thick ones for symmetry. | Practitioner-only (faithfulness inference) |

### Renderer rules (layout, visual, disclosure)

| # | Rule | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | **Minimise edge crossings,** especially between Links and tree edges. Route Links with hierarchical edge bundling. | Strong for crossings ([Purchase 1997](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/3-540-63938-1_67)); moderate for bundling ([Holten 2006](https://www.cs.jhu.edu/~misha/ReadingSeminar/Papers/Holten06.pdf)) |
| R2 | **Signal structure.** Visually distinguish Root Nodes, branch membership and Link vs. tree edge, through weight, size and colour. | Strong ([Schneider et al. 2018](https://www.learntechlib.org/p/204443/)) |
| R3 | **No decoration.** No clip-art, gratuitous images or ornamental curves by default. | Strong ([Mayer coherence](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-handbook-of-multimedia-learning/principles-for-reducing-extraneous-processing-in-multimedia-learning/F29A19FCD34C542806F736E0661C05F5)) |
| R4 | **Overview first, reader-driven disclosure.** Open on Root Nodes plus one level. The reader expands and collapses; nothing auto-plays. Keep about 15–25 Nodes visible per view. | Moderate ([Shneiderman 1996](https://hci.stanford.edu/courses/cs448b/papers/shneiderman96eyes.pdf); [Blankenship & Dansereau 2000](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/249038782_The_Effect_of_Animated_Node-Link_Displays_on_Information_Recall); [Ghoniem 2004](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/A-Comparison-of-the-Readability-of-Graphs-Using-and-Ghoniem-Fekete/4effb869de6edd5e6e7e1ec476ab402fa9d28c55); [Novak & Cañas](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~paritosh/papers/sketch-to-models/Novak-Canas-TheoryUnderlyingConceptMapsHQ.pdf)) |
| R5 | **Default to a linear tree layout** (left-to-right for long labels, or top-down) with siblings in generator order. Radial is at most an option. | Moderate ([Burch et al. 2011](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/6065011/)) |
| R6 | **Gestalt grouping.** Keep each branch spatially compact and separated from the others (proximity, symmetry, continuity). | Moderate ([Wiegmann et al. 1992, via O'Donnell et al. 2002](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1013132527007); [Krieglstein 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11423-022-10083-2)) |
| R7 | **Link visibility follows focus.** Draw Links only between visible Nodes. When a Link's endpoint is collapsed, show a badge or stub on the visible ancestor. Highlight a Node's Links on hover or selection. | Moderate (inference from R1 + R4; [Holten 2006](https://www.cs.jhu.edu/~misha/ReadingSeminar/Papers/Holten06.pdf) aggregates links onto ancestors) |
| R8 | **One categorical hue per Root branch,** which in practice also caps colour-coded roots at about 7. | Moderate ([Healey 1996](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/2639665_Choosing_Effective_Colours_for_Data_Visualization); [Ware](https://scholars.unh.edu/ccom/143/)); practitioner ([Buzan](https://www.mind-mapping.org/index.php?title=Tony_Buzan_has_defined_these_%22rules%22_for_Mind_Mapping)) |
| R9 | **Outline view as an equal alternative,** with Links shown as inline references. It serves strong verbal readers and experts (expertise reversal). | Moderate ([Nesbit & Adesope 2006](http://www.sfu.ca/~jcnesbit/articles/NesbitAdesope2006.pdf); [Kalyuga et al. 2003](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-007-9054-3)) |
| R10 | **Link labels are always rendered on the edge or on hover,** so a Link reads as a proposition. Visually distinguish relation types (e.g. dash style), not with more colours. | Practitioner-only ([Novak & Cañas](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~paritosh/papers/sketch-to-models/Novak-Canas-TheoryUnderlyingConceptMapsHQ.pdf); colour budget per R8) |

### Open questions for the spec

- No study measures **triage** speed or accuracy with Maps vs. prose for knowledge workers. The fixture-set eval in #20 checks coverage, not ease of consumption.
- Numeric thresholds (3–5 roots, 15–25 visible Nodes) are extrapolated from working-memory and node-link readability research, not tuned for Maps of AI-written Documents. Treat them as defaults to tune.
- The evidence on Link density is thin: one study says more interconnections reduce load ([Krieglstein 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11251-025-09736-5)), against a strong layout-clutter literature. The split (generator is generous, renderer is selective) is a design inference.
